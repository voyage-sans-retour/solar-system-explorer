Why did life evolve on Earth specifically?

This is the question of all time. What makes Earth special? The ingredients for life, carbon and hydrogen, are very common in our universe. It is likely that a great many planets host microscopic life. Complex life, however, is a different story.

Studies show that Earth has hosted single-celled organisms for about 3.5 billion years. Multicellular life did not appear until about 1 billion years ago, and relatively quickly complex organisms with familiar feautures began to populate Earth. Because of the suddenness, this event is known as the Cambrian Explosion. But why did it take so long, and happen so suddenly?

Science shows us that every complex organism on Earth, from planets to animals to humans, is made of complex cells called Eukaryotes. Simple organisms like bacteria, which are also the earliest organisms, are made up of Prokaryotes. Study of eukaryotic cells show that they have inside of them organelles that were once prokaryotes, but were one day fatefully swallowed up by another prokaryote and became a permanent part of it. This new complex cell was better able to store energy and prevailed throguh natural selection. Known as Endosymbiotic theory, this suggests that the Earth we know today is based completely on a fateful microscopic encounter.
