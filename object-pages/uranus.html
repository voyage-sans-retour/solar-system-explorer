Uranus

Quick Facts

  • Uranus is the seventh planet from the sun
  • It has an orbital period of 84 Earth years
  • It is named after the Greek god of the sky
  • Uranus has 28 known moons and also has some faint rings

Uranus' Discovery

Uranus is (barely) visible to the naked eye, and it was the first planet to be discovered with a telescope. Uranus' dim light and small size mademany people who had seen it before mistake it for a star or comet. Sir William Herschel, a German composer and amateur astronomer, was the first to bring it to attention after observing its movement through his handmade telescopes. Through mathematical calculations made by Johann Elert Bode, Uranus was confirmed to be a planet.

Herschel proposed to name Uranus Georgium Sidus, meaning George's star, after the King of England at the time. Other astronomers dissented and Uranus, Greek god of the sky, was decided upon instead.

FUN FACT: Uranus is nearly perpendicular to its orbital plane, making it appear to spin towards the sun like a rolling ball.

FUN FACT: Uranus is the coldest planet in our solar system, with temperatures reaching up to -224°C.
