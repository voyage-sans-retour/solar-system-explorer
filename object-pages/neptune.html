Neptune

Quick Facts

  • Neptune is the eigth and farthest planet from the sun
  • It has an orbital period of 165 Earth years
  • It is named after the Roman god of the sea
  • Neptune has 16 known moons and also has some faint rings

Neptune's Discovery

Neptune is the only planet not visible to the naked eye, and it was discovered the first planet to be discovered by math.

Galileo observed Neptune with his telescope in 1612 but marked it as a fixed star. Neptune was not discovered as a planet until much later, in 1846. Uranus had already been discovered, and astronomers had noticed irregularities in its orbit that made them predict that it was being affected gravitationally by another planet. Mathematician Urbain Joseph le Verrier spent years using mathematics to predict this planet's position, and when he was finished, he sent it to an observatory in Berlin. He was right on the dot, and a new planet was added to our solar system.

FUN FACT: Neptune has a similar axis tilt to its orbital plane, so it experiences seasons like Earth does. Each season on Neptune, however, lasts 40 years.

FUN FACT: Neptune had a famous storm on its surface called the "Great Dark Spot," similar to Jupiter. This storm was also larger than Earth, and it was photographed in 1989. This storm is gone now, though, and other ones have taken its place.

FUN FACT: Neptune is the windiest planet in our solar system, with supersonic winds reaching up to 2100 km/hr. Supersonic means winds on Neptune travel faster than sound in its atmosphere.
